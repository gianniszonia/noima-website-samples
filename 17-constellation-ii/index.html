Our name and symbol

A Greek word and the first analogue computer.

The name

νόημα

The Greek word for meaning

In Greek, when something makes sense, you say it has νόημα. Every business wants that from its data, so it became our name.

The symbol

A gear inside an open dial

The main wheel of the Antikythera mechanism, set inside an open dial that carries α, ω, the Moon and the Sun.

The two together

The name and the symbol as one

The symbol is the mechanism that read the sky. The name means meaning. Together they say what we do with your data.

The gear
The wheel at the centre, taken from the mechanism’s main gear. For us it stands for the machinery we build.
The dial
The open ring around the wheel, marked by four dots. On the mechanism, it is where the answer was finally read.
α to ω
The first and last letters of the Greek alphabet, top and bottom. From raw data to meaning, start to finish.
Moon and Sun
On the left and right, the two bodies the mechanism tracked. For us they are what you want to know.

Found in a shipwreck near Antikythera in 1901, a bronze mechanism of at least 30 gears predicted eclipses and the movements of the sky more than 2,000 years ago: the first analogue computer.

Questions

Common questions.

Our data is a mess. Is that a problem?

No. Tidying it is part of the job. The first step is working out what your data can support today.

Do you work with the tools we already have?

Yes. We start with what you have, spreadsheets included, and suggest a new tool only when the current one is holding the work back.

What can you automate?

Whatever your team does by hand on a schedule: sending invoices, building reports, running an analysis, sending emails.
